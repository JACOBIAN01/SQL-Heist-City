# Privacy Policy

**SQL Heist City** is a game in which students learn SQL. This policy says what we keep about students and teachers, why, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read by students, parents and schools. [Operator name and address to be filled in.]

## The short version
- Students sign in with a Google account, usually the one their school gave them.
- We keep a student's **name and email, a display name, which class they joined, and a record of the SQL questions they were asked and how they answered**. Nothing else about them.
- We do **not** keep a birth date, photo, address, phone number or location. We do not use cookies for tracking, show adverts or sell data.
- The school decides to use the game with a class and accepts these terms for its students.
- Students (and schools for them) can **download or delete** their data at any time.

## Who is responsible
The school that uses SQL Heist City with its students decides why and how the students' data is used (the "controller"). We run the game for the school and handle the data only on the school's instructions (the "processor"), under the Data Processing Agreement. [Operator] is responsible for the service itself. Contact: [privacy contact].

## What we keep, and why
| What | Why |
|---|---|
| Google account id, name and email; school domain | so that a student has their own account, and the teacher can see who is in the class |
| Display name (chosen by the student) | so players can be told apart in the game; names are checked against a word list |
| Classes joined | so a teacher can run a class and see its results |
| Each question a student was asked, their marks, how many tries and hints | to show the student and their teacher how they are learning (a profile of five skills and six levels of thinking), and to give questions that suit them |
| Briefs a student writes between rounds, with the teacher's marks and comments | so the teacher can mark open work |
| Whether the student said they are old enough (not the year they were born) | the game is for players of a minimum age (13 by default) |
| Technical records: session cookies, short logs of errors and cheating checks, numbers about server load | to keep the service working and fair; they hold no names, emails or answers |

The full list of every field is in our public data map. The student's own SQL is kept only while the match runs, for the review after the round, and is not stored in the database except in a brief they hand in.

## The game is violent
Students' characters shoot each other with guns and run each other over in a cartoon-style city while solving SQL questions. There is no blood or gore, and no way to play without the shooting. The school decides if this suits its students. [Age rating to be added when obtained.]

## Children
Students are often under 18 and sometimes under 13. We do not ask children for more than the list above. For students under 13 (United States) and the equivalent ages elsewhere, the school gives consent in place of a parent for educational use. We do not allow behavioural advertising, tracking of children, or sale of their data. Parents and guardians can ask the school, or us, to see or delete their child's data.

## Who else sees it
- **Google** receives a sign-in request and sends us the account's name, email and school domain.
- **Our hosting and delivery providers** store and serve the data for us under agreements that stop them using it for anything else. [Providers to be listed in the Data Processing Agreement.]
- **The student's teachers and the school's administrators** see the students in their own classes and their learning records. Other students see only the display name and game scores.
- We do not sell data, share it for advertising, or give it to anyone else unless the law requires it.

## How long we keep it
- A student account that has not been used for **a year** is deleted with everything attached to it.
- A class that a teacher archived is deleted **six months** later.
- Open briefs are deleted **two years** after they were handed in.
- Detailed answer steps are deleted after six months and summaries of finished questions after two years; the running totals behind a student's profile go with their account.
- Backups are kept for 14 days, so a deleted record can remain in a backup for up to that long.
These times are settings the operator can shorten.

## Your rights
A student, a parent or guardian, or the school can ask to **see, correct, download or delete** a student's data. Students can do it themselves from the menu ("Download my data", "Delete my account"). Under the law that applies to you (for example GDPR in Europe, COPPA in the United States, the DPDP Act in India) you may also object, restrict processing, or complain to the data-protection authority. Contact [privacy contact]; we answer within 30 days.

## Security
Connections are encrypted. Passwords are stored only as salted hashes, and sign-in sessions as hashes. Students sign in through Google, so we never see their password. Student SQL runs in an isolated sandbox that cannot reach other students' data. Access is limited and logged. If we learn of a breach affecting personal data we tell the school without undue delay, and within 72 hours where the law sets that limit.

## Cookies
We use cookies only to keep you signed in (a session cookie that lasts 12 hours) and, for a few minutes, during the redirect to Google. No tracking or advertising cookies.

## Changes
If this policy changes in a way that matters, schools are asked to accept the new terms again before a class can continue.
